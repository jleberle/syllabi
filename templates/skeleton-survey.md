# Grades

The course is worth **1000 points**. Nothing is weighted, curved, or converted — your grade is the number of points you have divided by 1000.

| Component                     | Points each | Given | Counted         | Total points | % of grade |
|:------------------------------|------------:|------:|----------------:|-------------:|-----------:|
| Syllabus quiz                 | 20          | 1     | 1               | 20           | 2%         |
| InQuizitives                  | 15          | 10    | 7   | 105          | 10.5%      |
| Source workshops              | 36          | 8     | 5   | 180          | 18%        |
| Note sheets                   | 15          | 3     | 3               | 45           | 4.5%       |
| Exam 1                        | 150         | 1     | 1               | 150          | 15%        |
| Exam 2                        | 200         | 1     | 1               | 200          | 20%        |
| Final exam                    | 300         | 1     | 1               | 300          | 30%        |
| **Total**                     |             |       |                 | **1000**     | **100%**   |

**Letter grades:**

|             | A        | B         | C         | D         | F         |
|:------------|:--------:|:---------:|:---------:|:---------:|:---------:|
| **Points**  | 895+ | 795–894   | 695–794   | 595–694   | below 595 |
| **Percent** | 89.5%+ | 79.5–89.4% | 69.5–79.4% | 59.5–69.4% | below 59.5% |

Final percentages are rounded to the nearest whole number before a letter grade is assigned, so 89.5% becomes 90% and an A. There are no curves on exams. A large portion of the grades here are assignments that are difficult to fail with the appropriate work on your part. All of the assignments besides the exams are a cushion to help make up for a lower exam score and not make the grade entirely reliant on timed exams.

Grades shown on Canvas are not official. The official grade is the one in my gradebook, and if you have concerns about your grade you should see me in student hours.

## Final Exam Replacement

If your percentage score on the final is higher than one of your first two exams, the final's percentage replaces the lower of the two. Example: 68% on Exam 1 (102/150) and 82% on the final rewrites Exam 1 as 123/150, a gain of 21 points. This is automatic and applies to one exam only; you do not have to ask, and it cannot lower a score. The replacement covers a zero for an exam you did not take. It does not replace a zero given for an academic integrity violation or for improper notes.

## Bonus Points

Two standing bonus point opportunities are offered: 25 points for completing every assignment that counts toward your grade and 25 points if 75% of the class completes the end of the semester evaluations. Completing every assignment means the syllabus quiz, all three note sheets and exams, at least seven InQuizitives, and at least five source workshops, with no zeros on the scores that count. Your dropped scores do not affect the bonus. The only other bonus I may offer is an in-class opportunity on a day of particularly low attendance (see Attendance). No other bonus points will be offered, please do not email asking for more.

## Early Exams, Makeups, and Late Work

**Known absences**: If you know you will be absent on a date an assignment is due, you must notify me as soon as you know and schedule a time to complete it before the date. Absences known about ahead of time are not grounds for an extension.

**Early exams**: If you cannot take an exam on its scheduled date, for any reason, you may schedule an early exam with me as soon as you know. I will do my best to find a time that works with my availability. If I do not have space or cannot find a compatible time, I will send you to the testing center, which charges a fee (currently $25) that you are responsible for. Exams taken through SAS are not charged.

**Unexpected absences**: Makeups are not offered by default for InQuizitives or Source Workshops as lowest scores are dropped. If an absence could not have been known ahead of time (for example, athletics or military training notifies you the same day that you are traveling), you may complete the work after the fact with verification that the absence was unexpected. If you are absent for an SAS-approved absence please contact me to arrange a makeup. Additionally if you are absent for an extenuating circumstance throughout the semester please see me during student hours to discuss alternatives.

**Exam makeups**: Exam makeups require verifiable third-party documentation showing you could not attend at the exam time; statements from you, family, or friends do not qualify. Contact me before the exam, or within 24 hours if contacting me beforehand was impossible.

**Resubmissions**: Once a grade is posted, the assignment cannot be revised or resubmitted to improve it.

# Individual assignments

Rubrics for every assignment, including the source workshops, note sheets, and exams, are posted on Canvas at the start of the semester.

### Syllabus Quiz

Ten questions at 2 points each, online, and open for the first week. Unlimited attempts; you may consult the syllabus while taking it. Submitting it constitutes your acknowledgment that you have read this document and understand its policies.

### InQuizitives

InQuizitives are assignments provided by Norton, tied to the textbook chapters on the schedule. There are ten and your three lowest scores are dropped. They are not a traditional quiz: wrong answers do not automatically cost you, and the activity adapts to what you know and continues until you reach the target I set for that assignment. Your grade is calculated automatically, and reaching the target earns the full score. These are meant to provide you an opportunity to work through material without the pressure of a timed, right or wrong style reading quiz. **InQuizitives are due by the end of the day on Friday of the week they are assigned.**

### Source Workshops

**Eight class meetings, dated on the schedule below, are source workshops.** Each is a ten-minute, handwritten, in-class writing exercise, completed on a handout provided in class, built around a single primary source — a song, a photograph, a film clip, etc. — that is not announced in advance. You will write one paragraph that makes an argument in response to a prompt, supported by the source and by evidence from lecture. These operate as mini-exams: on most workshops a bank of terms from lecture will be on the screen with the prompt, and you choose the terms that best support your argument. Some workshops will not have a term bank. Workshops are written without notes or devices, so review your notes from previous lectures before each date; a workshop covers material from lectures up to that day. Rubrics are posted on Canvas. **Your three lowest scores are dropped.**

### Note Sheets

For each exam you must bring **a standard 8.5"x11" one-sided page of handwritten notes**, containing whatever you want. That's right: exam notes are a graded component of the course: 15 for a detailed sheet, 8 for weak notes, 0 for none. Use of improper notes (typed, double-sided, oversized) will result in an automatic zero on the exam. If you attempt to cover up the use of improper notes you will be subject to a formal academic integrity hearing. **If you have a Student Accessibility Services (SAS) accommodation that affects how you can prepare or use a handwritten sheet** (for example, typed notes), see me in student hours before the first exam to arrange it. Formats approved through SAS are not subject to the penalty above.

### Exams

Three exams, all in class, handwritten in blue books, with your note sheet. Points breakdowns are available on the Canvas assignment for each exam. Besides your note sheet, no notes or other materials may be used on exams, and all electronics (phones, smartwatches, headphones, etc.) must be put away for the entire exam period.

**Exam 1 — 150 points**: Three IDs and an essay. Five IDs will be on your exam and you choose three to write; two essay prompts will be on your exam and you choose one.

**Exam 2 — 200 points**: Same as the first exam, except the point totals are increased.

**Final Exam — 300 points, finals week**: This exam runs for 150 minutes during finals week and has three sections. The first is IDs but unlike the first two exams you will identify three terms that relate to one another and write a paragraph identifying the terms and how they connect. The following two sections are essays, the first being a non-cumulative essay and the final section a cumulative essay.

#### Study guide

Prior to each exam I will distribute a study guide in class with the prompts that will be on the exam. The study guide will only be distributed in class on that day and will not be available on Canvas or after the fact. If you are not in class the day it is assigned you can get the guide from someone in class. If your absence is documented, you may instead pick up a copy from me in student hours with your documentation.

# Course Policies

## Attendance

**Attendance is not graded.** Coming to class is a critical component of succeeding in the course and a vast majority of your grade is directly tied to things done in class or connected to material covered in class lecture. Regular non-attendance will have a significant impact on your grade in the course. At the same time I want you to be in class during the semester because you're committed to learning the material, not because you want attendance points. Coming to class and sleeping, doing other work, or using your laptop to do anything besides notes will not improve your grade in the course. **I reserve the right to offer an in-class bonus point opportunity on a day of particularly low attendance.**
