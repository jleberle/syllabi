# Course Description

This course provides a general introduction to major themes and events in the history of North America and the United States beginning with early European-Indigenous encounters and continuing through the present day. Students must complete weekly lectures, assigned readings, and demonstrate acquired knowledge through exams. Students should expect to think critically about the complicated, and possibly controversial, nature of our nation's shared pasts.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the semester, you should be able to:

1. Explain major developments in North American and U.S. history from European-Indigenous contact to the present and situate them in chronological relation to one another.
2. Critically analyze a primary source: identify its author, audience, purpose, and limits as evidence.
3. Construct a written historical argument supported by specific evidence.
4. Evaluate competing historical interpretations rather than treating the past as settled.

## A Note About Online Delivery

This course is entirely online and asynchronous. Because this course does not meet in person, your work in the course each week will equal the time you would have spent in a traditional face-to-face class. Each week you will need to complete the assigned readings, lectures, and associated assignments.

I encourage you to contact me as soon as possible if there is any trouble comprehending the material or you find yourself falling behind or getting overwhelmed. I am also available to discuss constructing essays/planning for your exams.

# Individual assignments

### Syllabus Quiz

A ten question, pass/fail quiz covering the major parts of the syllabus and class requirements, due at the end of week one.

### Lecture Videos

Lecture videos are posted on the main page of the course: three each week (in week one, an introduction video and two lectures) and one on exam weeks, 39 in all. Each video watched in full earns full credit, each watched at least 50% but not in full earns half credit, and each watched less than 50% earns no credit. Credit comes from Canvas Studio's viewership data, which is the only record used. Videos must be watched the week they are assigned, by 11:59PM Central Time on Sunday, to earn points.

### InQuizitives

InQuizitives are assignments provided by Norton, tied to the textbook chapters on the schedule. They are not a traditional quiz: wrong answers do not automatically cost you, and the activity continues until you reach the point threshold for that assignment. Once you hit the threshold you can keep working for additional practice if you choose to. **InQuizitives are due by 11:59PM Central Time on Thursday of the week they are assigned.** Your four lowest scores are dropped.

### Exams

There will be four exams, each closed note and closed book, taken through ProctorU (see Exam Proctoring). Each exam is 90 minutes long, is worth 200 points, and has three sections, in this order:

- **Essay (100 points)**: Write a thesis-based essay responding to a prompt built from the lectures, supported by specific evidence from the course.
- **Source analysis (60 points)**: Analyze a primary source you have not seen before, using material from both the textbook and the lectures.
- **IDs (40 points)**: Six terms are provided, drawn from the key terms list in each textbook chapter, so you can build your own study list from those chapters. Choose four and identify each one and its significance.

**Each section locks once you complete it, and you cannot return to an earlier section.** Proofread and edit before moving on: treat each section as one you turn in before receiving the next. Plan your time by the points each section is worth: about 45 minutes for the essay, 25 for the source analysis, and 15 for the IDs, which leaves 5 minutes to spare.

Each exam is available in ProctorU from 9AM Central Time on Monday of the week it is due until 9:45PM Central Time on its due date (Sunday, or Wednesday for Exam 4). **The last time to start an exam is 9:45PM.** ProctorU does not allow a partial exam, so you cannot start one you could not finish by the deadline, and the 9:45PM cutoff also leaves room for check-in delays. Your lowest exam score is dropped.
